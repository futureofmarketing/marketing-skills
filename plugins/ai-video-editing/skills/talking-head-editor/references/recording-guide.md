# Recording Guide: One Good Take

The edit starts while you record. These habits make one take enough.

## Camera and picture
- **Record the camera, not your screen.** A screen recording with a small webcam bubble gives the editor a tiny, blurry face and may show private windows. Use the camera app (or your recording tool's camera-only mode).
- **Horizontal, 1080p or higher,** at a constant 30 or 60 fps, so there's room for visuals next to your face. The vertical Reel is made later from the same take.
- **Eye-level camera, light from the front,** and space around your head: a hand's width above your hair and your shoulders in frame. The editor crops in; it can't crop out.
- **A calm background** without bright windows behind you.

## Speaking
- **One idea per sentence.** Each sentence becomes one visual.
- **Name things one by one** ("images, logos, music"): every item you name gets its own animated element.
- **A short pause between sentences.** The editor cuts in the pauses.
- **Stumbled? Pause and repeat the whole sentence.** The editor keeps the last complete version.
- **Say your structure out loud** ("two things: first … second …"). It becomes the chapter map on screen.
- **Know your script, don't read it.** Bullet points beat a teleprompter.
- **45 to 90 seconds** is the sweet spot for a first video.

## Another language
- Record the same structure, sentence by sentence, in the same setting and framing. The editor then re-uses every scene and animation and only re-times them to your new take.

## Before you hand it over
Put into the folder any logo, screenshot or product image you want to see in the video, and a music track you have the rights to use. Say where the video will run (stage, LinkedIn, YouTube, website, Reels). The more precise your input, the closer the result gets to what you have in mind.
